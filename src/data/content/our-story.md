Sharav is the Hebrew word for the dry desert wind - the heat that sweeps across the region and leaves everything sharper once it has passed. The same wind is known everywhere it blows, by a different name in every language: *khamsin* in Arabic, *sirocco* across the Mediterranean, *ghibli* in Tunisia, *harmattan* in the Maghreb.

It belongs to no single people. It touches everyone. That is the whole idea behind this food.

Mizrahi means "Eastern" - the Jews of Iraq and Iran, Yemen and Syria, Morocco and Tunisia, Egypt and Kurdistan. Some of the oldest continuous communities on earth, and the reason this food tastes the way it does: it was never made in isolation. Ingredients, techniques and recipes moved in every direction, shared across faiths and borders, passed hand to hand.
