I'm the founder and chef behind Sharav, a Mizrahi-Jewish food project based in Gaithersburg, Maryland. My love of this cuisine started before I can even remember it: as a small kid at a Persian-run daycare, I was fed plate after plate of extraordinary food, and it never let go.

That early spark grew into something deeper than appetite. I spent years studying the Middle East, its history, its politics, and the peoples who have shaped and been shaped by the region. That grounding is why I approach this food the way I do: through the specific communities it comes from, with real attention to nuance and history, never flattened into a generic "Middle Eastern" label.

I reignited a serious passion for cooking after meeting my wife in 2024 and, true to form, chased it all the way down. A deep dive into my own genealogy, sparked by a family legend of an ancestor "buried between two boards in the sands of Algeria," had me wondering whether Mizrahi or Sephardic roots explained the pull I'd always felt. A DNA test settled it: 100% Ashkenazi.

By then it didn't matter. I'd fallen for the food and culture of Mizrahi Jews, the cuisine of North Africa, Yemen, and the Levant, and for the story it carries: communities displaced from their homelands who brought their food with them when they lost nearly everything else.

October 7th effected all Jews differently. For me, beyond the obvious profound sorrow and pain, I felt an ever-rising desperation for peace. Cooking a cuisine born of coexistence, food shaped over centuries by Jewish and Arab neighbors who shared space and ingredients, feels, in a small way, my insistence that shared humanity, and a shared meal, can be the first step toward peace.
